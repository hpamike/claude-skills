# Principle Heuristics

For each Cialdini principle, this file lists how it typically appears as Detective, Smuggler, or Commissioner on a marketing asset. Use these as starting points — the specific page will always have nuance the heuristics don't capture.

The central classification question for any tactic: **"What would have to be true for this to be a Detective use?"** If the answer is plausible and the page makes verification easy, classify Detective. If the answer is true but the framing obscures context, classify Smuggler. If the answer doesn't hold at all, classify Commissioner.

## Reciprocity

The norm that we return what we receive. The page gives something before it asks.

**Detective**
- Genuinely useful free content, tool, calculator, or template offered without immediate ask
- Trial that delivers real product value, not a gated demo
- Transparent "pay-what-you-want" or "free for students" offers

**Smuggler**
- "Free" that requires email, phone, and credit card upfront, framed as generous
- A stripped trial marketed as a "gift"
- Personalized discount that was always going to be offered ("just for you, 10% off" shown to every visitor)

**Commissioner**
- A "gift" that is not actually delivered until a purchase completes
- Pre-applied "coupons" that inflate the reference price

## Commitment & consistency

Small agreements make larger agreements more likely; people want to align with prior stances.

**Detective**
- Multi-step flow where each question clarifies the user's need and narrows the recommendation
- Onboarding that asks the user to articulate a goal and references it later when relevant
- Quizzes where the result is genuinely tailored to the answers

**Smuggler**
- Forced "pick one" answers where no option fits, manufacturing a stance the user didn't hold
- Progress bars that exaggerate completion to induce sunk-cost feelings ("you're 80% done!" on step two of five)
- Pre-checked consent boxes in a long form

**Commissioner**
- Confirm-shaming opt-outs ("No thanks, I don't want to save money")
- Fake "you said X earlier" personalization that the user never actually said
- Pretending a user made a commitment they didn't (e.g., "continuing implies acceptance" buried in microcopy)

## Social proof

We look to others — especially similar others — when uncertain.

**Detective**
- Real reviews with verifiable identities and source links (Trustpilot, G2, named customer)
- Customer logos the brand can prove it serves
- Statistics with a source and a date ("83% of users reached goal within 30 days, internal data Q3 2025")

**Smuggler**
- Curated testimonials with no last names, company, or context
- "Trusted by thousands" with no number
- Review feeds filtered to hide negatives
- "10 people are viewing this now" when the counter is approximate or averaged

**Commissioner**
- Fabricated reviews (AI-generated, purchased, or written by staff)
- Customer logos of companies the brand does not serve
- "X people just bought this" fake activity feeds that fire randomly
- "Used by top teams" with no actual top teams

## Authority

We defer to credentialed, experienced, or institutionally-marked sources.

**Detective**
- Real credentials, licenses, certifications displayed where verifiable
- Actual press coverage linked to the article
- Named experts with their real affiliations and contributions

**Smuggler**
- "As seen in [logo]" badges where the coverage was a paid placement or a passing mention
- Certifications that look regulatory but are self-issued or paid badges
- Expert quotes without full context or link to the source

**Commissioner**
- Fabricated press logos
- Invented awards ("Best in class 2024" with no issuing body)
- Lab-coat or stock imagery implying medical or scientific authority the business does not possess
- Fake expert endorsers

## Liking

We say yes to people we like. Similarity, warmth, and cooperation drive liking.

**Detective**
- Real team photos with names and roles
- Authentic brand voice consistent with the company's actual culture
- Founder story that is true and verifiable
- Customer community or events where staff genuinely participate

**Smuggler**
- Stock photos staged to imply a real team
- Generic "we love what we do" warmth that papers over a transactional relationship
- Personalization tokens ("Hi {{first_name}}, we picked these for you") on generic recommendations

**Commissioner**
- AI-generated "team members" or fabricated bios
- Invented founder story
- Fake friendly DMs from "the founder" sent to every new user

## Scarcity

We value what is rare, about to disappear, or competed for.

**Detective**
- Real inventory limits tied to actual stock
- Deadlines tied to real events (cohort start date, seasonal window, a contract with a supplier)
- Genuine waitlists with transparent mechanics

**Smuggler**
- "Limited time" offers that are effectively always running
- "Low stock" signals that reset per session or per user
- Countdown to a discount end that quietly renews
- Exclusivity claims ("members only") where everyone qualifies

**Commissioner**
- Countdown timers that reset on refresh
- "Only 1 left!" that always says 1, regardless of inventory
- Manufactured "lost your spot" warnings
- Fake waitlists that never gate access

## Unity

We say yes to members of "us." Shared identity, not merely similarity, including family, region, profession, cause, or community.

**Detective**
- Brand authentically serves a defined community (a profession, region, identity, cause) and its members self-identify with it
- Content, voice, and product decisions shaped by that community's actual needs
- Naming the community explicitly and inviting participation

**Smuggler**
- Co-opting identity language ("for builders," "for creators") without doing anything that specifically serves builders or creators
- Flag-waving or community-signaling in marketing that's absent from the product or policies

**Commissioner**
- Faked endorsement of or by an identity group
- Invented origin in a community the brand has no actual tie to
- Pretending to be owned by or built for a group it isn't
